## Why an AGPL project asks for a CLA

AgentCordon is licensed [AGPL-3.0-only](LICENSE), and it stays that way. A CLA
does not change that, and it does not take your copyright away.

What it does is keep the copyright in the project consolidated in one place. The
AGPL's network-use clause makes AgentCordon a poor fit for some organisations —
a company that wants to run it as part of a hosted service, or embed it in a
product it does not want to open-source, needs a different licence. Offering one
is only possible if a single party holds licensing rights over the whole
codebase. Without a CLA, the copyright in the project is spread across everyone
who has ever contributed, and every one of them would have to be tracked down
and agree before anything could be relicensed — including, in practice, a move
to a *more* permissive licence later, or a change forced by a legal problem in a
licence version.

So the grant here is what a commercial future needs and no more: the right to
sublicense and relicense your Contribution, plus a patent grant so neither the
project nor its users are exposed. You keep your copyright, your Contribution
stays available under the AGPL like everything else, and you remain free to use
your own work anywhere else on any terms you choose.

This is the same arrangement used by projects such as Grafana, Element and
MongoDB. If you would rather not sign, that is entirely reasonable — open an
issue instead, and the change can be discussed and, if it is a good one,
implemented independently.
