# Trademark Policy

**"AgentCordon", the AgentCordon name in any form, and the AgentCordon logo are
trademarks of the project's maintainer.** They are not licensed to you.

## The licence does not cover the name

AgentCordon's source code is licensed under
[AGPL-3.0-only](LICENSE). That licence grants rights in the *code*. It grants no
rights in the project's name, its logo, or its other brand assets — section 7(e)
of the GPL family explicitly permits this reservation, and it is the normal
arrangement for an open-source project.

So: you may copy, modify, run and redistribute the software as the AGPL allows.
You may not use the AgentCordon name or logo in a way that suggests your version
is the official one, or that the maintainer endorses it.

## Forks and derivatives must rename

If you distribute a modified version, give it your own name. Do not call it
AgentCordon, do not name it something confusingly similar, and do not ship the
logo.

Concretely, a redistributed fork should change:

- the product name shown in the admin UI and printed by `--version`;
- the binary names (`agentcordon`, `agentcordon-broker`, `agent-cordon-server`);
- the published container image name and repository;
- the banner and any icon derived from the AgentCordon logo.

You are welcome — encouraged — to say what your fork is based on. "Based on
AgentCordon" and "a fork of AgentCordon" are accurate statements of fact and are
fine. "AgentCordon Enterprise", "AgentCordon Cloud" or a logo with the
AgentCordon mark in it are not.

## Nominative use is fine

You do not need permission to use the name to refer to this project. All of
these are fine, and no attribution notice is required for them:

- Writing about, reviewing, comparing or criticising AgentCordon.
- Saying that your product, plugin, MCP server, chart or integration works with
  AgentCordon — "MyTool for AgentCordon", "AgentCordon-compatible".
- Naming it in documentation, talks, blog posts, courses and academic work.
- Linking to the repository, the documentation or the container image, and using
  the logo to link to the project unmodified.
- Running an unmodified build internally under its own name.

The test in every case is confusion: use the name to *identify* this project,
not to *brand* something else as it.

maintainer directly. Permission to use the marks beyond what is described here
